Maya runs a small landscaping operation out of a garage behind her rented cottage on the edge of town. When spring gets busy, she stops tidying the books and lets a shoebox of crumpled receipts pile up. Each month she sits at the kitchen table for five hours, a blunt pencil in hand, rewriting figures into a smudged ledger. The work eats the quiet end of Sunday night, and during peak season she falls behind. She bills $90 an hour when she is on a job site, so those five hours away from the lawn and hedge work carry a cost. This year she signed up for the bookkeeping service that costs $250 a month. The service categorizes transactions, reconciles the accounts, and sends a one-page summary by the 10th. With the books handled, she uses the five freed hours to take on extra planting and mulch jobs at $90 an hour, and Sunday evenings are hers again.

```
Hours freed for billable work: 5 — ASSUMED equal to time spent
Share of freed hours actually billed: 1 — ASSUMED
Net monthly gain = (5 × 1 × $90) − $250 = $450 − $250 = $200
```

What would change this:
